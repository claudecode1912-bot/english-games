Recipe book

Both tenses link the past with now. Present Perfect crafts a result: what we have now. Continuous crafts a process: what has been going on and for how long.

Present Perfect · the result

have / has+V3=I have finished

− She hasn't called.   ? Have you seen it?

  • Result nowI've lost my phone. (I don't have it now)
    результат важен сейчас
  • Life experienceHave you ever been to London? I've never tried sushi.
    опыт в жизни, без точного времени
  • Fresh newsThe match has just started. I haven't done it yet.
    только что / уже / ещё не
  • How many so farI've watched three episodes today.
    сколько уже сделано
  • States up to nowI've known Max since 2019.
    глаголы состояния не бывают Continuous
everneverjustalreadyyetso fartoday / this week

Present Perfect Continuous · the process

have / has+been+V-ing=I've been waiting

− He hasn't been sleeping.   ? How long have you been learning?

  • Started in the past, still goingI've been learning English for five years.
    действие длится до сих пор
  • Recent activity, visible tracesYour eyes are red. Have you been crying?
    видны следы недавнего действия
  • Repeated or temporary latelyI've been going to the gym a lot recently.
    в последнее время
  • How long, not how manyShe's been texting all evening.
    важен процесс и длительность
forsincehow longall daylatelyrecently

Same verb, different item

Present Perfect: resultContinuous: activity
I've built a house. Come and see it! (finished)I've been building a house. That's why I'm tired. (maybe not finished)
I've found three diamonds. (how many)I've been digging all morning. (how long)
She's read the book. (she knows the ending)She's been reading the book. (she's in the middle)

for + a period: for two hours, for ages · since + a starting point: since Monday, since 2020, since I was ten.

Two traps that break your pickaxe. 1) State verbs (know, like, love, want, believe, understand, have = own, be) don't take Continuous: I've known her, not I've been knowing. 2) A finished time (yesterday, last week, in 2020) means Past Simple: I saw it yesterday, not I've seen it yesterday.

Rules for the teacher
  • Correct = right tense, understandable: +1 resource. Perfect = right tense, full sentences, no mistakes: +2. Try again cracks the block, and it can be mined later.
  • Each task has a model answer behind the Teacher: show example button.
